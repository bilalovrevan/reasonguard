\section{Summary of Contributions}
\label{sec:conclusion-summary}

This thesis introduced \textsc{ReasonGuard}, a framework for runtime verification of
artificial-intelligence reasoning against formally verified bounds. The framework
treats the structured output of a formal automaton-based detector as a machine-readable
bound on what an AI explanation may correctly claim, and it classifies any crossing of
that bound into a taxonomy of five reasoning violation classes. The framework was
empirically validated on the IEC-104 dataset published by the NES@FIT group at Brno
University of Technology, and the empirical findings were translated into operational
deployment guidelines and mapped to the relevant provisions of the European Union AI
Act.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion-future}

\paragraph{Integration of the official NES@FIT automaton schema.}
The current bound layer uses a proxy classifier for the formal-class field. The
immediate next step is to replace the proxy with the official schema and to repeat
the evaluation against the upgraded bound.

\paragraph{Learning-based per-claim classifier.}
The per-claim three-state classifier is currently rule-based. A learning-based
classifier trained on the manually annotated set would close the precision-recall
trade-off observed in Section~\ref{sec:results-detector}.

\paragraph{Operator-in-the-loop user study.}
A study with operators would measure whether the V1--V5 severity classification
changes operator behaviour relative to the unguarded baseline. This is essential
before deploying \textsc{ReasonGuard} in production.

\paragraph{Multi-protocol generalisation.}
The framework is protocol-agnostic by construction. Generalisation to DNP3 and S7
would broaden the operational applicability and is the natural next contribution.
